## Map the battlefield before moving anything

Mark the castle, structures, unit rows, open firing paths, and the first point where enemy pressure arrives. Then identify which friendly unit must remain active for the plan to work. Formation begins with protecting that dependency while preserving its line of effect. A visually symmetric layout is not automatically safer if both sides share the same exposed weakness.

Use the first lost unit and the first blocked or missed action as diagnostic markers. If the key damage source falls first, the protection map is wrong. If it survives but contributes late, the angle or distance may be wrong. If the castle is exposed while the squad remains healthy, the movement or structure plan has failed even if individual units look strong.

## Account for row and adjacency behavior

Patch 1.16 explicitly describes heroes whose behavior depends on frontline position, a cat row, or the unit directly in front. That proves formation can alter a unit's function. For any placement-sensitive unit, record the required relationship in plain language and highlight the exact neighbor or row. Do not reduce the requirement to a generic 'front' tag when the official condition is more specific.

Test adjacency with one controlled move. Swap the sensitive unit or its required neighbor while keeping upgrades and opponents fixed. Compare whether the effect appears, who receives protection or damage, and what breaks elsewhere. This reveals the cost of activating a synergy, which is often more important than proving the synergy exists.

## Use movable castles and structures as geometry

The official site says castles can be moved and other structures can be placed. Treat these as geometry that changes paths, exposure, and available cover. Before moving the castle, predict which line becomes safer and which becomes vulnerable. After the move, check both. A change that solves one lane by opening another is a trade, not a universal improvement.

Place structures with a purpose label: protect, delay, channel, support, or create space. If a structure cannot be tied to the diagnosed failure, leave it unchanged during the test. This prevents decorative rearrangement from hiding the effect of the position that actually matters.

## A one-change formation test

Capture the starting layout, target, first contact, first loss, and castle condition. Move one unit, castle, or structure. Replay and record the same five observations. Improvement means the decisive failure happens later or changes category while the key function remains available. A different animation or a single lucky win is not enough to call the position solved.
