Write down the battle problem before opening the roster: early pressure, an exposed firing line, insufficient finishing damage, or a formation that cannot use its placement effects. ## Score fit without fake precision

Use a three-level assessment—clear fit, conditional fit, or unverified—across target relevance, survivability, placement compatibility, and upgrade burden. Avoid decimal scores when the underlying stats are unknown. A clear fit has visible behavior that addresses the named problem. A conditional fit needs a row, neighbor, structure, or resource level that the current formation may not supply.

Upgrade burden belongs beside combat fit. A unit that works only after consuming the next scarce tier may be a worse immediate choice than a weaker-looking unit already near readiness. Record the smallest upgrade required for the test and the resource you refuse to spend until the squad demonstrates a changed outcome.

## Build substitution chains

For every essential slot, name the function and two fallback paths: another unit with a similar observable job, and a formation or structure change that reduces the need for that job. Substitution is successful when the battle plan still works, not when animations look similar. If no alternative preserves the function, the slot is a real dependency and should receive progression priority.

Placement-sensitive heroes need an extra check. Patch 1.16 describes effects connected to the frontline, a cat row, and the unit directly ahead, so a substitute may change neighboring value even if its own damage appears adequate. Re-run the whole formation with only that slot changed and note which adjacent interactions disappear or activate.
